### Claude Discovers Novel CRISPR-Like Enzyme System in Phage Genomes: Anthropic Launches Wet Lab with "ART" Discovery
**Signal Purity Score: 98/100 • Autonomous Scientific Discovery Milestone**

For decades, the most transformative tools in biotechnology—from restriction enzymes and thermostable Taq polymerase to CRISPR-Cas9—were discovered through painstaking, serendipitous human observation of unusual microbial immune defenses. Today, foundation models officially entered the discovery loop as autonomous experimental researchers.

Anthropic officially announced the formation of a dedicated life sciences division and experimental wet laboratory in the San Francisco Bay Area. Accompanying the lab's public launch was a milestone finding: Claude foundation models autonomously identified a previously uncharacterized biological enzyme system named **Array-associated Reverse Transcriptases (ART)** in jumbo bacteriophage genomes.

To test whether frontier models could accelerate fundamental biology, Anthropic deployed an automated research harness of approximately 950 Claude agents running in parallel across 210 million tokens. The agents were instructed to analyze an uncurated database of over 200,000 bacterial and viral reverse transcriptases (RTs)—enzymes that transcribe RNA into DNA.

While inspecting the genomic neighborhood of an uncharacterized jumbo phage RT, an agent flagged a striking structural anomaly: *"[The DNA next to the RT] is spectacular: I can see by eye a tandem repeat array … that's a CRISPR-like … repeat array?!"* The agent measured the repeat distances, cross-referenced all existing biochemical literature, and proved that this non-coding array, together with an adjacent accessory protein, formed an entirely undescribed operon.

When Anthropic's wet-lab team synthesized and expressed the genes in vitro, RNA sequencing confirmed that the array is transcribed into distinct short RNA fragments, strongly suggesting an active, programmable targeting mechanism analogous to CRISPR guide RNAs. 

CRISPR pioneer **Professor Feng Zhang** of MIT and the Broad Institute validated the findings: *"This is an exciting example of how AI agents can contribute to biological discovery. The identification of RNA-repeat arrays associated with reverse transcriptases is genuinely intriguing and merits further investigation."*

- **What Happened:** Anthropic deployed 950 Claude agents that combed 200,000 reverse transcriptases to discover the novel ART enzyme system in viral phages, subsequently validated in Anthropic's Bay Area wet lab.
- **Why It Matters:** Marks the first empirical discovery of an uncharacterized biological enzyme system initiated and characterized autonomously by frontier AI agents.
- **What Could Happen Next:** Experimental trials will determine whether ART functions as an autonomous phage defense or a next-generation programmable RNA-to-DNA gene-writing tool.
