### 2. SMS of Death: Zero-Click Vulnerability in Android Telephony Drops Calls and Causes Boot Loops
**Signal Purity Score: 92/100 • Mobile Operating System Security**  
Security researcher Mark B disclosed 'SMS of Death', an unauthenticated zero-click Denial-of-Service flaw in Android's `WapPushOverSms.decodeWapPdu()` parser (`frameworks/opt/telephony`). A malformed 5-byte WAP Push payload (`88 80 80 80 00`) decodes to `0x80000000`, which is cast without bounds checking to signed 32-bit integer `-2147483648`, throwing an uncaught `NegativeArraySizeException` on `com.android.phone`'s main thread. The attack executes silently without user notifications, terminates active voice calls with `DisconnectCause CS_DEATH`, and blocks emergency Triple Zero (000) calls. Because carrier retry daemons resend unacknowledged messages and poison rows persist in `content://sms/raw`, affected handsets enter 90-second RescueParty reboot loops until factory reset or ADB purged. Google shipped vendor commit `735d967` in the October 2026 security release for Android 14 through 17.

### 4. SkyPilot Unveils Topology-Aware Scheduler for GB200 NVL72 Racks, Yielding 27% More Usable GPUs
**Signal Purity Score: 91/100 • AI Supercomputing Infrastructure**  
UC Berkeley researchers behind SkyPilot revealed that standard distributed schedulers cripple NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 performance by scattering multi-node jobs across racks, forcing critical tensor parallelism and MoE all-to-all dispatches off 1.8 TB/s NVLink onto 400 Gb/s InfiniBand (a 43% throughput penalty). Because a 72-GPU rack expands failure blast radius by 18x—failing every 5 hours on a 128-rack cluster—SkyPilot introduced Topology-Aware Scheduling (TAS). By sizing workloads into 16-node slices (64 GPUs) and reserving two in-rack hot-spares, clusters achieve 27% higher usable capacity under 2% hardware unavailability. In empirical trials with NVIDIA's GR00T robotics team on 512 GB300 GPUs, rack-aware scheduling accelerated pre-training throughput by 14.4%, saving $400K daily on 10,000-GPU clusters.
