### FAA Halts All U.S. Departing Flights After L3Harris Telecom Update Crash
**Signal Rating: 92/100 | Category: Technology & National Infrastructure | Primary Source: NBC News & The Register**  
*Read full analysis: https://nextvector.rhasan.online/article/faa-nationwide-ground-stop-l3harris-telecom-outage*

Only the third nationwide U.S. ground stop since September 11, 2001—following January 2023's NOTAM failure—stopped departures across all U.S. airports at 3:54 p.m. ET (20:54 UTC) on Friday, November 13, after communications faltered across high-altitude Air Route Traffic Control Centers. According to CBS News reporting cited by The Register, an update rolled out to L3Harris's FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure (FTI)—the private network connecting over 4,400 aviation facilities—caused the system to reboot and crash.

Key operational and forensic details from federal and industry sources:

1. **Passenger & Gate Cascade:** Flightradar24 tracked 4,042 flights bound for U.S. airports in the air at the moment of the halt. Dartmouth aviation logistics professor Vikrant Vaze estimated 3,000 to 4,000 domestic flights (out of roughly 25,000 daily) stalled during the initial hours, while a top aviation expert estimated 300,000 to 400,000 passengers were stranded. By 6:00 p.m. ET, Reagan Washington National hit full aircraft parking capacity and Chicago O'Hare approached capacity, while Newark LiveATC recordings captured controllers warning of severe gate shortages.
2. **Malicious Activity & Microsoft Overlap Ruled Out Initially:** DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin attributed the stoppage to a telecommunications outage with no early sign of nefarious activity, and three senior officials told NBC News investigators had found neither cyberattack signatures nor any connection to Microsoft's concurrent M365 disruption.
3. **Vendor Clarification:** Air Space Intelligence—awarded the FAA's $875 million, 12-year Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories (SMART) contract—issued a public statement on X confirming its software played no role in the ground stop.

### 1. Claude Helps Logicians Unlock 663 Theorems Inside Kurt Gödel’s Shorthand Notebooks
**Signal Rating: 91/100 | Category: Science & Formal Mathematics | Primary Source: The Guardian (Alex Bellos)**  
*Read full report: https://nextvector.rhasan.online/article/ai-deciphers-kurt-godel-shorthand-notebooks-coq-theorems*  
Working under a €2.5 million (£2.1 million) ERC grant at the University of Helsinki since 2017, logician Jan von Plato—one of roughly six living readers of obsolete German Gabelsberger shorthand—fed transcriptions of Gödel's 16 early-1940s notebooks (1,009 pages) and six monographs into Claude. In days, Claude produced a 650-page digest surfacing 663 candidate theorems across 14 unexplored themes in logic, accelerating a human pace of ~50 pages per year. Verified in the Coq proof assistant, ~400 results passed as stated, ~200 were repaired with tighter assumptions, ~50 proved false (echoing Gödel's marginal *falsch!* notes), and a near-complete proof of the independence of the axiom of choice passed formal checking—though Notre Dame logician Joel David Hamkins cautioned historians must clarify where Gödel stops and Claude begins.

### 5. Hopfield-Era Energy Model CRAM Solves Reasoning Puzzles 1,000x Faster Than Looped Transformers
**Signal Rating: 86/100 | Category: AI Research & Architectures | Primary Source: arXiv:2611.17309v1 (Prefrontal)**  
*Read full report: https://nextvector.rhasan.online/article/cram-hopfield-energy-model-outpaces-looped-reasoners*  
Researchers Benjamin Hoover, Hugh Blayney, and James C. R. Whittington at Prefrontal in London introduced CRAM (Compact Reasoning Associative Memory), replacing slow backpropagation through time in multi-million-parameter looped transformers with a learned 1980s Hopfield-Tank pairwise energy trained via local pseudolikelihood. On one H200 GPU, CRAM hit 100.00% across three seeds on Sudoku-Extreme (99.996% in a 42 ms single run) using 7.7K parameters and 2.02e9 FLOPs—training 1,000x faster and running with 282x fewer inference FLOPs than baselines like TRM and HRM—while compressing Maze-Unique to 18 nonzero weights. Crucially, the authors warn that puzzle saturation flatters toy benchmarks without reflecting scalability toward general reasoning suites like ARC-AGI.
